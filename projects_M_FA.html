Fly Away: 3lb Combat Lifter

A 3lb (beetleweight) control robot built for BotBrawl combat robotics competitions, utilizing a custom crank-and-rocker four-bar linkage mechanism.

Motivation & Strategy

Joining the University of Waterloo BattleBots design team (WATBOT) provided a hands-on opportunity to execute a full mechanical hardware lifecycle under strict weight and space constraints.

In BotBrawl (Canada's robot fighting league), matches are judged on damage, control, and aggression. Fly Away was designed explicitly as a control bot. The core strategy is to dominate control points by pinning opponents against walls or stranding them, using an active lifting arm to neutralize their weapons and control ring position.

Design Iterations

Version 1: Snail Cam & Extension Springs

The initial version attempted to store energy using a snail cam mechanism paired with heavy extension springs, aimed at launching opponents into the air.

  • Mechanism failure: Material deformation and improper component placement caused the spring to pull the entire flipper structure upward rather than releasing kinetic energy through the arm.
  • Anime North 2026: Due to a tight 2-week manufacturing timeline, tolerance stack-up on the cam and over-stiff springs prevented proper deployment. The weapon was removed hours before the competition, converting the bot into a static wedge to gain driver experience.

Version 2: Crank-Rocker Linkage (Current)

The design transitioned from a flipper to a high-torque crank-and-rocker four-bar linkage lifter. Linkage kinematic simulation software was used to tune arm ratios to maximize lifting height and mechanical advantage.

  • Modular Attachments: Implemented interchangeable front attachments: forks for vertical spinner opponents, and wedges for horizontal spinners to prevent weapon damage.
  • Gear Reductions: Driven by a dedicated high-torque gear motor mated with a 1:2 spur gear reduction to clear internal chassis geometry.
  • Drive & Geometry: Shifted to rear-wheel drive with extended front caster wheels to prevent tipping forward when lifting 3lb opponents.

Material Selection & Architecture

Materials were specifically chosen to balance severe weight constraints with structural impact resistance against high-speed spinning weapons:

Subsystem Component Material Selection Design Rationale
Outer Shell & Wedges TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane) High impact absorption, flexibility, and crack resistance against weapon hits.
Bottom Plate Carbon Fiber Provides extreme rigidity and structural strength at minimal weight.
Top Plate Tegris Woven Composite Lightweight, highly impact-resistant armor protecting top electronics.
Flipper Uprights UHMW Polyethylene Slick, rigid material supporting high-stress arm pivot points.
Inner Supports & Mounts ABS / Super PLA+ Dimensional stability and customizable 3D printed geometry.
Arms & Forks Aluminum / AISI 304 Stainless High strength-to-weight ratio for primary load-bearing linkage arms.
Wedge Plate Ti-6Al-4V Titanium Exceptional hardness and strength-to-weight ratio to deflect low blades.

Key Learnings & Competition Results

Competing at the BotBrawl Summer Classics validated the overall mechanical improvements, while exposing critical areas for future iteration:

  • Design for Serviceability (DFM/DFA): Version 1 required a hyper-specific teardown sequence. Version 2 reduced top-plate hardware by 66%, featured quick-snap side armor, and an independently removable lifter cassette.
  • Wire Management: Embedded dedicated internal wire routing paths into CAD to prevent loose cables from snapping solder joints during high-G impacts.
  • Identified Failure Modes: Lack of an inverted self-righting mechanism remains a vulnerability when flipped. Bluetooth latency via ESP32 control showed a need to transition to standard 2.4GHz RC protocols.
