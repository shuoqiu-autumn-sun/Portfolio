48V-12V Buck Converter

A DC-DC step-down buck converter board designed to convert a 48V input down to a stable 12V output rail for high-power system components.

Since I am still fairly new to PCB design, the project description is formatted as a Q&A to document the questions I had while reviewing the datasheet, selecting components, and completing the layout.

Schematic and Component Selection

The TI TPSM65630 (P65630SVCGR) was selected because its operating parameters match the target input voltage range.

What is a low-impedance connection?

  • A connection typically ranging from 2 to 600 Ω.
  • It allows high current transfer with minimal power loss and voltage drop.

Why are there two VIN pins if they are tied together?

  • Splitting current load (thermal management): Sharing the current across two pins reduces contact resistance and distributes heat evenly.
  • Internal circuit separation (noise cancellation): Separates noise-heavy high-power switching transistors from sensitive analog control logic to prevent switching noise from interfering with feedback loops.
  • Silicon layout and geometry: Optimizes internal die spacing and silicon trace routing.
  • VIN1 and VIN2 are tied together externally and connected to an input capacitor array.
  • C_IN (Bulk Capacitor): Serves as a local energy reservoir, supplying rapid current demands when internal switches turn on to prevent input voltage dips.
  • C_HF (High-Frequency Decoupling Capacitor): Filters out fast, high-frequency voltage spikes that bulk capacitors cannot suppress due to parasitic inductance.

What capacitor types are best for high-power applications?

  • Multilayer Ceramic Capacitors (MLCCs): Selected for their low Equivalent Series Resistance (ESR) and low Equivalent Series Inductance (ESL), enabling efficient noise filtering without power delivery bottlenecks.
  • X7R/X7T Dielectrics: Maintain structural stability and capacitance across high temperature and high voltage ranges.

What is a precision enable pin (EN)?

  • An input pin that enables or disables the IC at an exact, predictable voltage threshold (typically within 2% to 3% tolerance).
  • EN is connected to VIN as specified in Table 5-1 (Pin Functions) of the datasheet.

If VCC originates from an internal LDO, why is it broken out to a pin, and why is the LDO needed?

  • The internal LDO steps down high input voltages to a safe operating level for internal control circuits.
  • The external pin allows decoupling to complete the required circuit loop (input, output, and ground reference) and stabilize internal supply rails.

Why use two 22 µF capacitors in parallel for COUT?

The datasheet specifies a minimum COUT of 15 µF. Ceramic capacitors experience capacitance loss under DC bias voltage, so two capacitors are placed in parallel to:

  • Halve the Equivalent Series Inductance (ESL).
  • Reduce DC bias capacitance degradation compared to using a single larger package.
  • Improve thermal dissipation across multiple components.

The feedback resistor values (RFBT and RFBB) were determined using Table 7-2 in the datasheet.

How is the timing resistor (RT) value calculated?

  • Target switching frequency (FSW): 1800 kHz (selected from Table 7-2).
  • Calculation formula: $RT\text{ (k}\Omega\text{)} = \frac{16.4}{FSW\text{ (MHz)}} - 0.633$
  • Calculated value: $8.4781\text{ k}\Omega$; selected the closest standard resistor value ($8.45\text{ k}\Omega$).

What is the difference between AUTO and FPWM modes on the MODE/SYNC pin?

  • AUTO Mode: Reduces switching frequency at light loads to maximize power efficiency.
  • FPWM Mode (Forced PWM): Maintains a fixed switching frequency regardless of load, delivering predictable output ripple and lower noise.
  • Selected FPWM mode for EV applications to maintain deterministic signal behavior.

Resistor power ratings were selected with a 2x safety margin over expected power dissipation.

While the PGOOD pin typically interfaces with an external microcontroller, an onboard MOSFET-driven LED indicator circuit was added to provide visual status indication for standalone operation.

PCB Layout and Routing

Input Capacitors & Feedback

  • Input capacitors are placed symmetrically and as close as possible to the VIN pads, using direct copper pours on the 48V net to minimize loop impedance.
  • The feedback resistor divider is placed directly adjacent to the IC feedback pin as recommended by the datasheet.

General Layout Conventions

  • Implemented solid copper pours for power and ground planes to minimize resistance and inductance.
  • Routed standard signal lines using trace widths between 0.2 mm and 0.3 mm.

If wider traces lower resistance and inductance, why not make all traces wide?

Trace width is selected based on current capacity, allowable temperature rise, voltage drop, and impedance requirements.

Extending copper pours to every trace introduces unnecessary parasitic capacitance, which negatively affects high-speed signal integrity.

For power and ground planes, inter-plane parasitic capacitance acts as a distributed high-frequency decoupling capacitor that stabilizes supply rails. However, on signal traces, excess capacitance forces signal drivers to charge and discharge larger parasitic loads, slowing edge transitions and degrading signal quality.
