Self-Balancing Robot

A two-wheel self-balancing robot using MPU6050 IMU and PID control on Arduino Uno

🤖
Status ✅ Complete & Tested
Duration 4 Weeks
Team Solo Project
Category Control Systems · Robotics
🤖

📋 Overview

The goal of this project was to build a two-wheel self-balancing robot from scratch. The robot uses an MPU6050 IMU to measure its tilt angle and angular velocity, then applies a PID control algorithm to keep itself upright.

This project was a great introduction to control systems, sensor fusion, and real-time embedded programming. The challenge was tuning the PID gains to achieve stable balancing while also making the robot responsive to external disturbances.

🔧 Component Selection

Every component was chosen carefully to balance cost, performance, and ease of use:

  • Microcontroller: Arduino Uno — simple, well-documented, and fast enough for real-time control
  • IMU: MPU6050 — 6-axis accelerometer + gyroscope with built-in DMP for sensor fusion
  • Motors: 2x NEMA 17 stepper motors with encoder feedback for precise speed control
  • Motor Driver: L298N — simple dual H-bridge capable of driving both motors
  • Power: 12V LiPo battery with voltage regulator for stable power delivery
  • Chassis: 3D-printed PETG frame for durability and light weight
  • Wheels: 65mm diameter rubber wheels for good grip and traction

💡 Design Decisions

Several key decisions shaped the final design of the balancing robot:

  • Stepper vs DC Motors: Stepper motors were chosen for their precise position control without the need for encoders, simplifying the control loop.
  • PID on Angle vs Angular Velocity: The control loop uses a PID controller on the measured tilt angle, with the derivative term acting as damping on the angular velocity.
  • Sensor Fusion: The MPU6050's DMP was used to combine accelerometer and gyroscope data into a single, stable tilt angle estimate.
  • Low-Pass Filtering: A complementary filter was added to smooth the sensor readings and reduce noise in the control loop.
  • Chassis Design: The center of gravity was kept as low as possible by mounting the battery at the bottom of the frame.

⚡ Challenges & Learnings

The most challenging part of this project was PID tuning. It took several days of trial and error to find the right combination of gains that would balance the robot without it oscillating wildly. The key learning was that the derivative term (Kd) is crucial for damping out oscillations, while the proportional term (Kp) determines how aggressively the robot responds to tilting.

Another challenge was power management. The motors required a lot of current during startup, which would sometimes cause the Arduino to reset. Adding a separate voltage regulator for the logic circuit solved this issue.

🏆 Results

The robot successfully balances on two wheels and can recover from small disturbances (like gentle pushes). It can also handle slight inclines and can be controlled to move forward and backward while maintaining balance.

The project demonstrated a solid understanding of PID control, sensor fusion, and real-time embedded systems.
